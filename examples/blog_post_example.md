# How to Spot Air Duct Cleaning Scams Before They Waste Your Money

The most reliable way to spot an air duct cleaning scam is to ask for NADCA certification before anyone enters your home. **NADCA-certified technicians** must pass the ASCS examination and use commercial-grade source removal equipment. The low-price coupon operations that dominate Facebook groups have neither. If the company cannot produce a NADCA membership number, treat that as the first red flag.

## What Makes Air Duct Cleaning Scams So Common?

Air duct cleaning scams are common because the service is invisible. Homeowners cannot see inside their duct system, which makes it nearly impossible to verify whether any work was actually performed or performed correctly. Unscrupulous operators exploit this information gap.

The economics make it attractive for bad actors. A van, a shop vac, and a door-hanger printer are the only startup costs required for a fraudulent operation. **No license, no certification, and no actual equipment** are needed to collect payment from homeowners who do not know what legitimate duct cleaning looks like.

Several recurring tactics drive the majority of consumer complaints:

- **The whole-house coupon** - advertised at suspiciously low prices in Facebook groups and on door hangers; the low price is a door-opener, not a real quote
- **Fake mold discovery** - technician "finds" black mold without any laboratory testing and upsells a high-margin treatment
- **Scope inflation** - arrives to do one area, then claims the entire system needs cleaning and presents a dramatically higher invoice
- **Unverifiable credentials** - claims NADCA certification or "EPA certification" that does not exist under that name

## When Should Rockford Homeowners Call a Professional vs. DIY?

You can safely handle basic duct maintenance yourself, but anything requiring access inside the duct system needs a certified professional. Here is where the line falls.

### Safe checks you can do yourself

- **Inspect your registers** - remove covers and look inside the boot with a flashlight; visible debris clinging to the duct wall is a sign cleaning is overdue
- **Check your filters** - a filter that loads within two weeks is a signal that particulate concentration is high in your system
- **Smell the supply vents when the furnace runs** - musty or dusty odor during first heat of the season is a meaningful indicator
- **Look for visible mold near return air grilles** - surface mold at a return grille can indicate a deeper moisture problem worth investigating

### Red flags that require a licensed expert in Rockford

- **Carbon or soot staining** around supply registers - indicates combustion gases entering the duct system, a safety issue beyond cleaning
- **Collapsed or disconnected flex duct** - visible only with a camera; requires physical repair before cleaning
- **Confirmed water intrusion in plenum** - requires professional remediation and source inspection, not just cleaning
- **HVAC system performance decline after prior duct cleaning** - may indicate the previous cleaner damaged internal components or disconnected a branch
- **Visible black growth on plenum surfaces** - requires laboratory identification before treatment; never treat assumed mold without testing

## How Does Professional Legitimate Duct Cleaning Actually Work?

A legitimate air duct cleaning follows the NADCA ACR 2013 standard for source removal, which requires specific equipment and a documented process that you can verify before, during, and after the job.

**Step 1: Pre-cleaning camera inspection.** A flexible camera is inserted into the supply and return trunks before any equipment is connected. You see the interior of your ductwork on a screen before cleaning begins. This documents the baseline condition and identifies any structural issues.

**Step 2: Negative air machine connection.** A commercial-grade vacuum with HEPA filtration is connected to your main supply trunk. This creates negative air pressure inside the duct system, meaning all air and dislodged debris flows toward the vacuum rather than into your living space.

**Step 3: Agitation and source removal.** Technicians move branch by branch through every supply and return run, using rotating brushes and compressed air tools to dislodge material from duct walls while the negative air machine maintains suction throughout the system.

**Step 4: Post-cleaning verification.** A second camera pass confirms the duct interior is clear. You receive documentation of both passes side by side.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Air Duct Cleaning Scams in Rockford

### How can I verify that a duct cleaning company is actually NADCA certified?

You can verify NADCA membership directly at nadca.com using the company's name or ZIP code. **NADCA maintains a public member directory** that is updated in real time. Any company claiming certification that does not appear in that directory is misrepresenting their credentials. You can also call NADCA directly at 1-855-NADCA-NOW to verify a membership number.

### Is it safe to let a duct cleaning company into my home if they cannot show certification?

No. Allowing an uncertified company access to your duct system carries real risk beyond the financial. **Unqualified technicians can physically damage flexible ducts**, disconnect branch runs, or use equipment that blows debris into your living space rather than extracting it. Insist on NADCA certification and proof of liability insurance before any technician enters.

### What should I do if I already paid a duct cleaning company and suspect it was a scam?

If you paid by credit card, file a chargeback dispute with your card issuer immediately and document your reason. If you paid by cash or check, file a complaint with the **Illinois Attorney General's Consumer Protection Hotline at 1-800-386-5438**. Also report the company to the Better Business Bureau so other Rockford homeowners see the complaint record.

### Can a duct cleaning company test for mold on the spot during a cleaning appointment?

No. **Legitimate mold identification requires laboratory analysis** of a physical sample sent to an accredited environmental testing lab. Visual identification during a duct cleaning is not a valid mold diagnosis. Any company that claims to have found mold and offers to treat it immediately without a lab report is using a well-documented upsell tactic.

### How do I know if the duct cleaning I already had done was actually completed properly?

If you did not receive before-and-after camera documentation, you have no verifiable proof that source removal was performed. **The only reliable way to verify a past cleaning** is to schedule a new camera inspection, which a legitimate company will perform. If interior duct surfaces still show heavy particulate adhesion to walls, the previous cleaning did not meet NADCA source removal standards.
