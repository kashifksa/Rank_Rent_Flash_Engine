### Do I need a permit for air duct cleaning in Illinois?

No, residential air duct cleaning does not require a permit in Illinois. It is a maintenance service, not a mechanical modification. **No permit is required** by the state or by Winnebago County for standard duct cleaning, duct sanitization, or dryer vent cleaning on existing duct systems. Permit requirements only apply when new ductwork is installed or an existing system is substantially altered.

### What happens if I delay air duct cleaning in my Rockford home?

Postponing air duct cleaning allows particulate buildup to accumulate in your supply and return trunks, reducing airflow efficiency and increasing the allergen load circulated through your living space. In Rockford's older homes with galvanized ductwork, **uncleaned ducts accelerate furnace filter loading**, which raises energy costs and shortens blower motor life. Mold risk increases if any condensation or moisture has entered the duct system.

### How do I know the duct cleaning was actually done completely?

Every job includes a video camera inspection before and after cleaning. **Before-and-after camera footage** is your proof that the work reached the interior of your trunk lines and not just the visible registers. You receive documentation of both passes. This is the NADCA-recommended verification method and the primary way to distinguish a complete cleaning from a surface-level service.

### Are the ultra-low-price whole-house duct cleaning coupons legitimate?

No. A complete residential duct cleaning that meets NADCA source removal standards requires commercial-grade negative air equipment, a camera inspection system, and trained technicians. **These services cannot be delivered at the prices advertised in Facebook group coupons or door hangers.** Offers at those levels consistently involve bait-and-switch tactics where the low price is a door-opener and the actual scope or final invoice is dramatically different. Always ask for NADCA certification and a written scope of work before scheduling.

### What does NADCA certification mean for duct cleaning?

NADCA stands for the National Air Duct Cleaners Association. **NADCA-certified technicians** have passed the ASCS examination and are trained to perform source removal cleaning according to the ACR 2013 standard, which is the benchmark for complete duct cleaning in the industry. Hiring a NADCA-certified company is the most reliable way to verify that a duct cleaner uses legitimate methods and equipment.

### How often should air ducts be cleaned in a Rockford home?

NADCA recommends air duct cleaning every three to five years for most households. **Rockford homeowners in pre-1985 homes** or households with pets, smokers, recent renovation work, or occupants with asthma or allergies should consider cleaning every two to three years. If your home has never had professional duct cleaning, schedule an initial inspection regardless of the timeline.
